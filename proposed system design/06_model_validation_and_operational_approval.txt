Phase 6. Model validation and operational approval

This is a theoretical design assuming that LIMITATIONS.md and certain nuances are addressed. The earlier phases make a corrected identity traceable to a snapshot, a policy version, and the code that wrote it. This note states what would have to be true before the loss definition and the intended use are approved. It is not that approval. The recorded pounds remain directional outputs under stated assumptions.

The only use that can be approved here is a batch reading of a closed window, for sites the caller may see. Gross and netted stay separate. E, F, H, and J are shown with their identity strings. I appears only when the excess count and the fit allow it. The reader does not add the rows, does not rank them, and does not treat them as a recommendation to hold a service percentile.

Outside that use, and not approvable here: a regulatory value-at-risk, a capital number, a live control tower, a write-back to the warehouse system, a treatment effect for reroute or safety stock, and a Hawkes-modulated year. The portfolio mitigation fractions remain arithmetic on a count.

History, before a pack is assembled, is at least four closed quarters on the first site. Each quarter is its own run, bound to the snapshot hashes and the policy then in force. Both views are attempted. A netted block for a missing cancel ledger is a recorded outcome. It is not replaced with the public-file max-pick and then called history. A quarter that blocked for a missing clock does not count as published. A change of seed list or of the holding divisor is a new policy version and a new comparison. Restore and rollback have been exercised in the same window. An in-flight run at restore was marked for resubmission and was not resumed.

Four quarters support a sensitivity narrative. They do not create power. Coverage tests on one year of weekly windows remain weak, and the pack says so.

Uncertainty is the spread across the stored seed list: minimum, tenth, ninetieth, and maximum, beside the mean. That spread is not a second F. The single seed 314159 remains the reproducibility lock. Each published quarter also records the dragon count, the active-day count, whether the coefficient-of-variation cap bound, the currency exclusion count, the coverage ratios, and the flags for a thin panel, a failed seed, and serial dependence. Dependence does not change the functionals. It is disclosed because extreme-value standard errors under dependence are optimistic. I, when withheld, is reported with the excess count. It is not imputed. G is reported as not requested. No interval is drawn around a sum of functionals, because the sum is not a column.

The calibration object is the hit test: a sum of line loss against an empirical rolling sum of the same column. F and H are not that test. The pack reports the window count, the unconditional likelihood ratio and its tail, including the zero-hit case, and the independence and conditional-coverage statistics only when they are identified. It states the loss definition, so a reader can see whether holding sat on both sides. It does not report a p-value of 1, and it does not use the words calibrated or stable. With about 34 windows at 5 percent, zero exceptions have expectation near 1.7. Zero exceptions are unsurprising. The portfolio hit counts are not acceptance numbers. Calibration is not described as observed until two closed quarters have been scored on the civil-date grid. If independence is unidentified in every quarter, the pack says there is no observation. It does not borrow a portfolio p-value.

Sensitivity is a new run under a new policy version, not an edit of the original row. The holding divisor 365 is compared with 1. E and F do not move, because they exclude holding. Line loss and the hit test move only when holding is in the loss definition. Margin is compared with the commercial owner's alternate; E, F, and H scale, they are not re-estimated. The confirmed cycle-time cut is compared with the unconfirmed sampler cuts of 240 and 360, to show how many dragons the wrong cut would mark. A value-threshold dragon rule, if run, is marked mechanical. The stress-day count 252 is compared with 365. Neither is an expectation. Regime cuts are compared with the owner's replacement, and empty slices are reported as empty. No sensitivity multiplies E by a mitigation fraction.

Approval is a signature on the pack. The commercial owner signs the loss definition, the margin, the holding divisor, and the stress-day count for the stated use. The KPI owner signs the clock pair, the reconcile tolerances, the coverage floor, and the regime cuts actually used. The risk engineer signs the seed list, the withholding rules, and the statement that I and the hit test were not asked to do more than their definitions allow. A missing signature does not approve. Migration signatures on the bootstrap policy do not count. Approval does not rewrite published runs. A later change of loss definition is a new policy, a new run, and a new pack.

Beside the signatures, and not a substitute for them: the image was built in CI, rollback and restore have been exercised, a trace carries the run identity, and the security and failure-path tests pass. An incident is a run that cannot compute, or a dependency that cannot accept work. A large expected shortfall is a result.

Approval does not mean a 99th-to-95th service effect has been estimated from telemetry. On the synthetic path that language is a configured delay band. On the observed path it is a pair of attainment levels, and days between them sit in neither slice. It does not mean clustering has been shown to be small. It does not mean a daily fit with four excesses is an annual 95 percent value-at-risk. It expires for a new site, a new clock, or a new loss definition.

Phase 6 is complete when the pack exists and the three roles have signed it or recorded a refusal. A refusal is a finished outcome. No observed figure is described as approved without the signatures.
